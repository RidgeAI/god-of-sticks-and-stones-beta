# LONG BOOK DESCRIPTION
## The God of Sticks and Stones
### Scripture, Creation, and the Quiet Language of God

---

## LONG DESCRIPTION (400–600 words)

Before humanity raised towers, it touched the ground.

The biblical story begins not in marble halls or imperial courts, but in a garden. Dust. Breath. Trees. Rivers. Stone. Seeds. The people of scripture lived close to creation — digging wells, watching stars, following flocks, reading the land for hidden water, and sleeping beneath open skies. Their encounter with God happened repeatedly in these grounded places: beside wells, under trees, in wilderness caves, upon bare mountains, among shepherds and farmers and laborers.

*The God of Sticks and Stones* is a contemplative exploration of the symbolic language woven through scripture and the created world. Tracing the recurring patterns of ordinary creation — seeds, stone, water, trees, fields, wilderness, and the quiet labor of humble people — this book follows one of scripture's most persistent theological movements: that the kingdom enters history not through spectacle and scale, but through hidden, grounded, ordinary things.

Moving through eighteen meditative chapters, the manuscript charts a four-part journey from Eden to Restoration. In Part I, the foundations are laid: the garden as origin, trees as witnesses, stone as memory and foundation. In Part II, the shadow of Babel rises — brick replacing stone, towers reaching upward while wells dry up, and the wilderness stripping away every illusion of self-sufficiency. Part III enters the theological heart: mountains and caves, the moving tabernacle, the carpenter working quietly with wood — and the cross, where all the manuscript's symbols converge. Part IV turns toward restoration: the rejected cornerstone revealed, the living field, the faithful laborer, the river returning, and finally the restored city where garden and civilization are no longer enemies.

At each stage, the created world speaks. Seeds preach burial and resurrection. Rivers preach descent before life. Roots preach hiddenness before fruitfulness. Stone preaches witness, foundation, and permanence beyond human ambition. And the shepherd — who walks slowly, knows the flock individually, and leads toward water rather than driving toward productivity — becomes one of scripture's deepest images of the kingdom's kind of power.

This book does not argue against civilization, knowledge, or human creativity. It concerns itself with something older and quieter: whether humanity has slowly lost the ability to notice. In a world organized around speed, noise, scale, and manufactured identity, the contemplative patterns of scripture and creation can become nearly inaudible. This book is an invitation to slow down and listen again — not to some secret or esoteric wisdom, but to what creation has been saying all along, in the language of roots and rivers, dust and breath, sticks and stones.

The manuscript maintains a non-reactionary, compassionate posture throughout. It does not look down upon the world but moves toward it — much like the laborers Christ sends into the harvest, who enter the field not to condemn it but to cultivate within it. The harvest, after all, is people. All thirsty. All fields capable of bearing fruit.

*The God of Sticks and Stones* will resonate with readers drawn to contemplative theology, biblical symbolism, creation spirituality, and the tradition of meditative reflection found in writers such as Eugene Peterson, Frederick Buechner, Wendell Berry, and Thomas Merton. It is a book for those who sense that the modern world has become too loud — and who still hope to hear the quiet things.
