# RECOMMENDED METHODS

## Option 1 — Chapter Epigraphs

Each chapter opens with:
- one key verse.

Example:

Insert occasional scripture directly into prose.

This preserves flow while increasing grounding.

- scripture references,
- thematic notes,
- symbolic pathways.

This allows readability without interrupting cadence.

# PHASE FIVE — LINE EDITING

# OBJECTIVE

Refine sentence-level writing.

This phase matters enormously.

The manuscript’s power depends heavily upon:
- rhythm,
- pacing,
- cadence,
- and restraint.

---

# PRIMARY LINE EDITING GOALS

## 1. REDUCE REPETITION

Important concepts should recur.
Identical phrasing should not.

Repeated terms to monitor:
- communion,
- rootedness,
- systems,
- modern civilization,
- downward before upward,
- fragmentation.

---

## 2. IMPROVE CADENCE VARIATION

The short-line poetic style is effective.

However,
continuous short-line structure can flatten impact.

Future edits should occasionally include:
- fuller narrative paragraphs,
- descriptive grounding,
- scriptural scenes,
- sensory detail.

This creates breathing room.

---

## 3. REMOVE OVER-EXPLANATION

Some sections currently:
- explain symbolism multiple times,
- restate already established themes,
- or linger too long on single concepts.

Future editing should trust the reader more.

Strong symbolism often works best when:
- lightly held,
- suggestive,
- and spacious.

---

## 4. PRESERVE QUIETNESS

Do not over-intensify the manuscript.

Its strength lies partly in:
- stillness,
- humility,
- patience,
- and reflective pacing.

Avoid editing toward:
- dramatic sensationalism,
- emotional manipulation,
- or aggressive prophetic language.
