## 1. CLARITY

Ensure readers can:

- understand transitions,
- recognize theological grounding,
- and move naturally through the manuscript.

It means:
- navigable depth.

## 2. RESTRAINT

The manuscript is strongest when:
- suggestive rather than forceful,
- invitational rather than argumentative,
- and contemplative rather than declarative.

Future edits should reduce:
- over-explanation,
- repetitive emphasis,
- and unnecessary intensity.

---

## 3. RHYTHM

The prose depends heavily upon:
- cadence,
- pacing,
- silence,
- and movement.

Future edits should preserve:
- breathing room,
- tonal variation,
- and emotional flow.

---

## 4. COMPASSION

The manuscript must never drift toward:
- superiority,
- contempt,
- fear-driven messaging,
- or condemnation.

The reader should continually feel:
- invited,
- understood,
- and accompanied.

The narrator remains:
- fellow laborer,
not:
- distant judge.
