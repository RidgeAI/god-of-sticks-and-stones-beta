## THE CLARIFIED DESTINATION

*As stated by the author:*

The manuscript's destination is NOT attentiveness, contemplation, mindfulness, or noticing creation. These are pathways, not destinations.

The manuscript's destination is:
- communion with God
- relationship with God
- partnership with God
- rediscovering the meeting place that was never lost

Supporting theological claims established:
- God has always been speaking
- God has always been near
- Humanity became distracted
- Slowing down does not create God's presence — it allows recognition of presence already there
- Creation points beyond itself toward the Creator
- The ordinary world is not the destination but the meeting place
- Communion naturally reshapes orientation and life
- The movement is primarily God toward humanity, not humanity toward God

## SECTION 1: ARCHITECTURAL ALIGNMENT

### The Core Question

Does the sequence Chapter 17 → Chapter 18 → Closing Reflection deliver communion and partnership with God — or does it accidentally terminate at attentiveness and observation?

---

### Chapter 17 — The Final City and the Restored Garden

Chapter 17 is the most theologically explicit chapter in the manuscript regarding communion. It names the destination clearly and without ambiguity.

Key passages from the chapter:

> *"From Eden onward, the deepest human need was always communion. Not merely survival. Not merely knowledge. Not merely advancement. Presence."*

> *"Adam was placed in the garden to tend and keep it. Partnership was always the design."*

> *"Work itself becomes redeemed. No longer driven by fear, scarcity, or pride, but integrated into communion."*

> *"For generations, the temple represented the meeting place between God and humanity. But in the restored city, no temple is needed. Presence fills everything. No veil remains. No separation remains. No hidden holy place remains. Communion becomes open again."*

> *"In Eden, God walked with humanity."*

> *"Humanity was always searching for this city — not merely a place of safety, but a place where communion, creation, and humanity finally dwell together without fear again."*

Chapter 17 communicates the destination with precision: communion restored, partnership resumed, meeting place open without temple or veil. The chapter arrives at the manuscript's true endpoint and names it explicitly.

**Architectural function:** Chapter 17 describes the destination from its eschatological fulfillment — the vision of what communion fully realized looks like. It is the horizon. It is correct. It is necessary.

**Architectural limitation:** It operates at the eschatological register. The reader sees the destination from a distance, in its final fulfilled form. They are not yet placed inside it in the present moment. The New Jerusalem is not this Tuesday's kitchen. The reader understands where the road leads; they have not yet been told they are already walking in the meeting place — that the veil-torn reality of Chapter 17's vision is already partially available to them in the ordinary room they are about to return to.

---

### Chapter 18 (condensed) — The God of Sticks and Stones

Chapter 18 returns from the eschatological horizon to the elemental. It argues that God has always spoken through ordinary things — stick, stone, seed, field, water, tree. This is the manuscript's title vindication and its method statement.

The critical theological question: Does Chapter 18 point THROUGH creation toward the Creator (creation as medium), or does it STOP at creation (creation as endpoint)?

**Answer:** Chapter 18 points through. "And the God who has always spoken through them" — the final line correctly identifies God as the agent and creation as the instrument. The elements are the medium; God is the speaker.

**Architectural gap:** The chapter ends at the declaration of God's method — God speaks through these things. It does not name the purpose of that speaking: communion, relationship, partnership. The declaration is true and necessary. But the declaration alone leaves the reader with the method without the destination. The chapter says: here is where God has always spoken. It does not say: and God has been speaking because God desires to meet you there.

**Structural risk:** The element list (Stone. Tree. Water. Seed. Dust. Breath.) is the most rhythmically powerful passage in the chapter. It is more memorably cadenced than the final prose sentence. A reader whose attention is absorbed by the element list may receive "Stone. Tree. Water. Seed. Dust. Breath." as the emotional landing rather than "And the God who has always spoken through them." The creation-as-endpoint risk is structural as well as theological.

**Invitation passage (Passage 9 — in condensed chapter):** The bullet list — "faithful laborers, rooted trees, people willing to receive living water, builders who remember the cornerstone, and stewards humble enough to walk with God through the field" — is the chapter's most explicit partnership language. "Stewards humble enough to walk with God through the field" is the closest any existing text comes to naming the content of the meeting: walking with God, in the ordinary field, as the form that communion takes in daily life. This passage exists but is framed as an invitation to "become again" — it names the partnership as a possibility rather than as a present reality already available.

---

### Alignment Verdict: The Current Sequence

The current three-part sequence moves:

**Ch. 17:** The eschatological vision of communion fully restored — horizon, destination, description of the meeting place at its fulfillment.

**Ch. 18:** The elemental declaration of God's method — creation as the medium through which God speaks. The ordinary as the location where speaking happens.

**Closings A/B/C:** Various mixtures of permission, re-attunement, and presence — none of which complete the transit from method to meeting.

**What is missing from the transit:** The sequence establishes:
1. WHERE the meeting will ultimately be (Ch. 17 — restored city, everywhere, no temple)
2. WHERE God has always been speaking (Ch. 18 — through ordinary things)
3. That the reader may return to ordinary life (Closings A/C — permission)

What the sequence does not yet complete:
- *You are already standing in the meeting place.* Not the eschatological fulfillment — but the anticipation of it. The ordinary room is where the meeting happens now, because the veil has already torn, because God descended rather than requiring human ascent.
- *The meeting is this* — walking with God through the ordinary field, returning to the work as a co-laborer, receiving the day as the field in which communion happens.

**Architectural verdict:** The sequence currently terminates between the method (Ch. 18 — God speaks through things) and the destination (Ch. 17 — communion fully restored) rather than completing the circuit by placing the reader in the meeting place now.

The closings are attempting this completion but are not achieving it. Closing B risks terminating at attentiveness. Closings A and C approach the meeting place without fully entering it. The reader is brought to the door consistently and then left there.

## SECTION 2: THE MEETING PLACE TEST

*The author describes the manuscript as "setting up the meeting place and showing the reader where the meeting place always was."*

Each component is evaluated against three questions:
- Does it point toward the meeting place?
- Does it enter the meeting place?
- Or does it stop short?

---

### Chapter 17

**Points toward:** YES. Chapter 17 describes the meeting place at its eschatological fulfillment — the city where communion, creation, and humanity dwell together; where no temple is needed because presence fills everything; where God walks with humanity as in Eden.

**Enters:** NO — by register. The eschatological vision is the destination; the chapter stands at its edge looking in. The reader sees the meeting place fully realized. They are not yet inside it in the present moment.

**Stops short:** By design. The eschatological vision is the correct register for Chapter 17. Its function is to establish the FORM and CONTENT of the meeting place. The transit to the present moment is the closing reflection's job.

**Critical detail for the closing:** Chapter 17 contains one passage that is the theological key to the meeting place in the present: *"No temple is needed. Presence fills everything."* This is not only an eschatological claim. It is also the explanation for why the ordinary room is already a meeting place — the movement from temple-as-meeting-place to presence-fills-everywhere is already inaugurated in the torn veil of Chapter 10. The ordinary room is already the location of divine presence because the temple separation has already been removed. Chapter 17 establishes this. No closing reflection completes the transit from that truth to the reader's present location.

---

### Chapter 18 (condensed)

**Points toward:** PARTIALLY. The ordinary things — stone, tree, water, seed, the commute, the kitchen — are identified as where God speaks. This is pointing toward the meeting place: the ordinary is where the meeting happens.

**Enters:** NO. The chapter ends at declaration ("And the God who has always spoken through them") rather than at encounter. The meeting place is pointed to; the meeting itself is not enacted or named.

**Stops short:** YES. The gap is between: God speaks through ordinary things (method statement) and: and God speaks through them because God desires to meet you there (communion statement). The method is established. The purpose of the method — communion, relationship, meeting — is not named in the ending.

---

### Closing A — The Laborer's Return

**Points toward:** YES. The ordinary work, commute, kitchen are named as where God is present. "The one who worked with his hands... has always been willing to meet you in the ordinary hours." The meeting is located in the ordinary.

**Enters:** PARTIALLY. The reader is sent back to the meeting place with God present in it. The laborer returns to the field where God is. But the nature of the meeting — communion, co-labor, walking with God — is implied rather than named. The reader enters the ordinary as a laborer; they do not explicitly enter it as someone entering a meeting.

**Stops short:** SLIGHTLY. The meeting place is named and the reader is sent into it. What happens inside the meeting — the relationship, the communion, the partnership of ordinary life — is not completed.

---

### Closing B — The Tuning Fork

**Points toward:** PARTIALLY and AMBIGUOUSLY. The stone, the sound, the wind — these point toward ordinary creation. But they point toward creation's testimony without pointing through it toward the Creator. The "speaking" that continues is not identified as God speaking through things; it is described as an attribute of the world.

**Enters:** NO. The reader attunes to creation. No encounter is named. No God is named. No meeting happens in the reflection; the reader is calibrated to perceive something. Calibration is not meeting.

**Stops short:** YES — and the shortfall is the most significant of any component. Reflection B stops before the meeting place. It stops at: the world is speaking and you can hear it again. The meeting place is the location where the speaker meets the hearer. Reflection B does not reach that location.

---

### Closing C — Experimental Version

**Points toward:** YES. The room is named. God is present in it. The invitation remains open. God has always preferred that kind of company. The meeting place is the room; the God who seeks ordinary people is already there.

**Enters:** PARTIALLY. "He has always preferred that kind of company" positions God as desiring the meeting. "The invitation has not closed" positions the meeting as available. But the content of the meeting — what communion involves, what the reader is invited into — is not named. The reader stands at the threshold with the door open and God's desire for their company established. They have not yet stepped through.

**Stops short:** SLIGHTLY — and this is the smallest shortfall of any component. The threshold is fully reached. The door is confirmed open. God's desire is established. The one remaining gap is: what is on the other side of the door.

---

### Meeting Place Test — Summary

| Component | Points Toward | Enters | Stops Short |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter 17 | Yes — eschatological form | By design — register is horizon | By design |
| Chapter 18 | Partially — names the medium | No — declaration, not encounter | Yes — method without purpose |
| Closing A | Yes | Partially — reader enters as laborer | Slightly — meeting not named |
| Closing B | Partially and ambiguously | No — attunement, not meeting | Yes — most significant shortfall |
| Closing C (experimental) | Yes | Partially — at threshold | Slightly — inside unnamed |

## SECTION 3: CREATION VS. CREATOR

### The Central Risk

The manuscript could leave readers attending to the medium rather than encountering the One who speaks through it. Creation's testimony is preparatory. The meeting with the Creator through creation is the destination. These are not the same.

---

### Chapter 17

**Creator-focus:** Strong. God is named throughout. Communion is the explicit destination. The New Jerusalem is defined by God's presence ("And God will dwell with them"). The city's light is divine; its river flows from the throne. Creation is present (river, tree, fruit, stone) but clearly as participant in God's dwelling rather than as destination in itself.

**Risk:** Low. The chapter explicitly names communion with God as the deepest human need and the city's purpose.

---

### Chapter 18 (condensed)

**Creator-focus:** Partial. "And the God who has always spoken through them" correctly identifies God as the agent. The elements are the medium; God is the speaker. But the structure places the element list before the God-sentence. The cadence peaks at "Stone. Tree. Water. Seed. Dust. Breath." and then the God-sentence arrives as conclusion.

**Risk:** Structural. The element list is more rhythmically powerful than the sentence that frames God as the speaker. A reader carried away by the cadence may register the elements as the emotional landing rather than the God-sentence as the claim. This is not a theological failure in the text — the text is correct — but it is a structural risk for a reader who receives the cadence before the content.

---

### Closing A

**Creator-focus:** Good. "The one who worked with his hands, who ate with people who weren't certain they belonged at the table, has always been willing to meet you in the ordinary hours." God is present through action (worked, ate, met). The focus is relational and participatory. Creation is not foregrounded; the work and the companion in the work are.

**Risk:** Low. The reflection is oriented toward the laborer and the companion (God), not toward creation as endpoint.

---

### Closing B

**Creator-focus:** Absent. God is not named. The speaking is impersonal — "the speaking continues," "it was waiting for you to get quiet." The stone, the sound, the wind are the specific images. A reader who receives Reflection B without the prior seventeen chapters could understand it as: *the world has an inherent quality of testimony; contemplative attentiveness allows perception of it.*

**Risk:** High. This is the most significant creator-vs-creation risk in the entire closing architecture. The reflection correctly preserves the idea that creation speaks continuously. It does not preserve the idea that creation speaks because the Creator speaks through it, and that the Creator desires to be found.

The distinction is not subtle. "The speaking continues" is a different claim from "God has not stopped speaking through ordinary things because God has not stopped seeking you." Reflection B communicates the first claim. The manuscript requires the second.

---

### Closing C (experimental)

**Creator-focus:** Strong. God is named explicitly and early. "And God will be there." The meeting is personal: wells, table, road — three historical moments of personal encounter. "He has always preferred that kind of company" names God as a desiring person, not an impersonal speaking quality. The invitation is God's.

**Risk:** Low. The reflection is oriented toward God as person, not creation as testament. The room contains God; the room is not the destination.

---

### Creator vs. Creation — Summary

| Component | Creator-Focused | Creation Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 17 | Strong | Low |
| Chapter 18 | Partial — structural risk | Moderate |
| Closing A | Good | Low |
| Closing B | Absent | High |
| Closing C (experimental) | Strong | Low |

**Overall finding:** The primary creator-vs-creation risk is concentrated in Closing B. If Reflection B is selected as the final closing, the manuscript risks ending in a contemplative-attentiveness posture directed toward creation's testimony without naming the Creator who speaks through it and seeks the reader. This would misrepresent the manuscript's destination.

## SECTION 4: PARTNERSHIP ANALYSIS

The manuscript's recurring imagery — laborer, steward, co-worker, walking with God through the field — positions the reader as an active participant alongside God in the ordinary work of ordinary life. Partnership implies mutual activity and shared direction; it is not passive reception or observation.

---

### Chapter 17

**Partnership language:** Present and explicit. "Adam was placed in the garden to tend and keep it. Partnership was always the design." "Work itself becomes redeemed. No longer driven by fear, scarcity, or pride, but integrated into communion." "The laborer, the builder, the shepherd, the farmer, the craftsman — all are restored into proper relationship."

**Register:** Eschatological. The partnership described is the fulfillment of the original design — restored, healed, fully realized. Chapter 17 shows partnership as the manuscript's ultimate vision but does not connect it to present-tense ordinary Tuesday.

---

### Chapter 18 (condensed)

**Partnership language:** Present in the invitation passage. "Faithful laborers." "Stewards humble enough to walk with God through the field." "Builders who remember the cornerstone." These are the strongest partnership images in the condensed text. "Stewards humble enough to walk with God through the field" is the single most precise description of the content of the meeting: the meeting involves walking together, in the ordinary field, as co-laborers.

**Limitation:** Framed as invitation to "become again." Partnership is a possibility the reader is invited toward, not a present reality they are being placed within.

---

### Closing A

**Partnership:** Strongest of the three reflections. "So go back to the work. Quietly, and with purpose — the way a laborer returns after a long rest." The reader is a laborer. The return is active. God is present in the commute, kitchen, and work.

**Gap:** The co-labor dimension is implied but not stated. The reader returns to the work; God is present in the work. That God and the reader are co-laboring — that the ordinary work is partnership in the field — is not explicitly named. The reader participates; whether they participate WITH God in a shared direction is left for implication.

---

### Closing B

**Partnership:** Essentially absent. The reader got quiet and heard something. There is no work, no labor, no tending, no co-labor. The posture is contemplative reception. "That is enough" affirms the contemplative posture as sufficient — which, for the purposes of the manuscript's destination, is insufficient. Attentiveness is preparatory; the manuscript's closing requires participation.

---

### Closing C (experimental)

**Partnership:** Present but unnamed. The invitation remains open — but to what? "He has always preferred that kind of company" establishes God's desire for the reader's presence. But the content of what the reader is invited INTO is not specified. What does the relationship involve? What does walking with God through the field look like? The invitation is open; the room on the other side of it is dark.

**Gap:** Partnership requires named participation. "The invitation has not closed" is not partnership language; it is threshold language. The reader is at the door. Partnership begins inside.

---

### Partnership Analysis — Summary

| Component | Partnership Present | Posture Reader Is Left In |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 17 | Explicit | Eschatological vision of restored partnership |
| Chapter 18 | Present (invitation passage) | Invited to become co-laborers (possibility) |
| Closing A | Strong participation | Active laborer returning to work with God present |
| Closing B | Absent | Contemplative observer calibrated to hear |
| Closing C (experimental) | Present but unnamed | At the threshold of an unnamed relationship |

**Overall finding:** No existing closing reflection names the content of the partnership. Closing A places the reader in the right posture (laborer, active, returning to work with God) but does not name it as partnership. Closing B bypasses partnership entirely. Closing C brings the reader to the threshold without naming what is inside.

The partnership gap is not that the theme is missing from the manuscript — it is everywhere in Chapters 17 and 18. The gap is that no closing reflection completes the transit: *the ordinary work you are returning to is the field; walking with God through that field is what communion looks like in ordinary Tuesday.*

## SECTION 5: THE READER'S FINAL UNDERSTANDING

*"After finishing this book, I believe the author wants me to understand that..."*

---

### Current architecture (manuscript ends at Chapter 18, no closing reflection)

"...God speaks through ordinary things in creation — stone, tree, water, seed — and that these elemental symbols carry meaning modern life has taught us to overlook. The deepest human questions are answered not through abstraction but through creation's living language. I should slow down and pay more attention to these things."

**Risk:** Creation-focused. Method-focused. Terminates at attentiveness. No communion named. No meeting place entered.

---

### Architecture with Closing A

"...God speaks through ordinary things, and the faithful response is to return to ordinary life without shame or loss. The laborer who goes back to work is doing something theological. God meets me in the commute, the kitchen, the work that will still be there tomorrow morning. He did not require spectacle from the people he met along the road. He is not requiring it now. I have permission to be ordinary. I was always enough to carry it."

**Assessment:** Better. Adds permission, presence, and companionship. Approaches participation. Still does not explicitly name communion or meeting place. The God-and-reader-together dimension is implied but not completed. Partnership is approaching; the reader is sent back to the right location, with God present, without being told they are walking into the meeting place.

---

### Architecture with Closing B

"...The world has been speaking all along, and I had grown too distracted to hear it. The language of creation is still present — in stone, in wind, in ordinary sounds. I got quiet briefly, and that was enough. The speaking will continue whether I attend or not."

**Assessment:** Most at risk. Creation-focused. Attentiveness-affirmed as sufficient. God not named. Communion absent. Partnership absent. A secular reader could receive this interpretation without remainder. The manuscript's governing theology — that the speaking continues because the Creator speaks through creation toward humanity, seeking communion — is not present in this landing.

---

### Architecture with Closing C (experimental)

"...God is in the room I'm about to walk back into. Not waiting for a better version of me. He preferred being with ordinary, unprepared people — at wells, at tables, on roads. He prefers that company still. The invitation to that kind of relationship has not closed."

**Assessment:** Closest to the clarified destination. God named and present. Historical initiative demonstrated. God's desire established ("preferred that kind of company"). Meeting place identified (the room). Invitation stated. Missing: what the relationship involves on the other side of the invitation. The reader arrives at the door knowing God is there and God wants their company — but what happens inside the meeting is unnamed.

## SECTION 6: FINAL RECOMMENDATION

### Ranked Non-Negotiable Elements

*The elements that must exist in the final ending so the reader leaves understanding the clarified destination. Ranked by damage to the manuscript's theological integrity if absent.*

---

**1. Communion is available now — not eschatologically, not after improvement, but in the present ordinary moment**

This is the destination. Everything else exists in service of this. The reader must leave knowing that what Chapter 17 describes as the ultimate vision is already partially available — that the veil has torn, that the temple is no longer needed, that presence fills the ordinary room they are returning to. Without this, the ending delivers the vision without the transit, the method without the meeting.

*Currently present in:* No closing reflection — this is the primary gap across all three.

---

**2. God desires relationship and desires it with this reader as they currently are**

The desire dimension is what separates communion from mere accessibility. God is not simply available if the reader approaches — God is oriented toward the reader. God preferred ordinary, unprepared company. God moved first at the well, the table, the road. God moves first still.

*Currently present in:* C (experimental) most strongly. Partially in A. Absent in B.

---

**3. The ordinary world is the meeting place — not the pathway to the meeting place, not a symbol of the meeting place, but the actual location where God meets ordinary people**

This is the manuscript's architectural claim made pastoral. The ordinary room is where God prefers to be. The commute, the kitchen, the work — these are not spaces the reader passes through on the way to encounter; they are the location of encounter. The meeting is not behind the symbols; the meeting happens through them.

*Currently present in:* Implied in A and C. Not stated explicitly in any reflection.

---

**4. God is near in the specific room the reader is returning to**

Makes the meeting place concrete rather than abstract. Not "God is omnipresent" but "God is in that room." The specificity is essential — without it, divine nearness remains a theological claim rather than a present pastoral reality.

*Currently present in:* C (strongest). A (implied). B (absent).

---

**5. Partnership — the ordinary work is the field; the return to ordinary life is co-labor with God**

The meeting is not passive reception. The reader returns to ordinary work as a laborer in the field where God is also at work. The partnership that Chapter 17 describes as the original design and the eschatological restoration is already available now, in the ordinary, through the ordinary work of ordinary life.

*Currently present in:* A (strongest, approaching partnership). Absent in B. Unnamed in C.

---

**6. The invitation remains open — permanently, without deadline, without qualification**

Removes urgency. Removes the pressure of a closing moment. The reader is not required to respond right now; the meeting place will still be there. God's desire for the reader's company is not time-limited.

*Currently present in:* C ("The invitation has not closed"). Partially in A. Absent in B.

---

**7. God has always been speaking — through creation, through ordinary things, continuously**

Provides historical continuity. The reader is not initiating something new; they are recognizing what was already happening. God's speaking is prior to human attention; the attention allows recognition of what was always occurring.

*Currently present in:* B (strongest). Implied in A and C. Stated in Ch. 18.

## THE FINAL QUESTION

### Has the manuscript identified its true ending destination? Or is there still a missing cornerstone?

**The destination has been identified.** The author's clarification — communion with God, relationship with God, partnership with God, the meeting place that was never lost — is the correct destination. The manuscript is built to reach this destination. Chapters 1 through 17 construct the architecture; Chapter 18 seals the method; the closing reflections are meant to complete the transit.

**There is still a missing cornerstone.**

---

### The Missing Cornerstone: The Nature of the Meeting

Chapter 17 contains a passage that is the theological key to the entire closing architecture, but no closing reflection engages with it:

> *"For generations, the temple represented the meeting place between God and humanity. But in the restored city, no temple is needed. Presence fills everything. No veil remains. No separation remains. No hidden holy place remains. Communion becomes open again."*

This passage establishes the form of the meeting place: not a sacred space set apart, not a special category of experience, not a spiritual discipline that must be practiced to open access — but ordinary presence, everywhere, because the veil has been removed. Presence fills everything.

The manuscript's entire argument about creation's ordinary language — stick, stone, seed, field, water, tree — arrives at this point. The reason creation speaks is not that creation is inherently luminous. Creation speaks because God speaks through it. And God speaks through it without temple, without veil, without hierarchy of sacred versus ordinary — because communion has been made open again.

**The cornerstone that is missing from every closing reflection is this:** *The ordinary room is already the meeting place — not because the reader has prepared for it, not because the reader has slowed down and attuned, but because the veil has already torn, because the temple is no longer needed, because presence already fills the ordinary hours. The meeting place is not the destination the reader is walking toward. It is where the reader already stands.*

This is not the same as "God is present everywhere" (which is doctrine). It is: *the ordinary room you are returning to is where the meeting always was, and God is already in it, preferring your company, desiring communion, ready to walk with you through the ordinary field.*

The three closings collectively say: God is present (C), the reader can return without shame (A), the speaking continues (B). None of them say: *you are already standing in the meeting place; you don't need to find it.*

The difference is this:

- "God is present in your ordinary life" — presence claim
- "Your ordinary life is the meeting place, and God is there seeking you" — communion claim

The manuscript has built eighteen chapters toward the second claim. The closing reflections consistently land on the first.

---

### What the Cornerstone Requires

The missing cornerstone does not require length. It does not require theological explanation. It requires one clear gesture — in the closing reflection — that completes the transit from *God speaks through ordinary things* (Ch. 18's ending) to *and the ordinary place where you stand is where the meeting has always been, and God is there, preferring your company, ready to walk beside you in the field.*

Chapter 18 ends: *"And the God who has always spoken through them."*

The closing should complete: *through them — to reach you; because the meeting was always here; and God has always preferred this kind of company; and the invitation has not closed.*

The cornerstone is not a new idea. It is present in the manuscript. Chapter 17 establishes it (no temple needed; presence fills everything). Chapter 18 gestures toward it (God has spoken through ordinary things). The invitation passage of Chapter 18 names the partnership ("stewards humble enough to walk with God through the field"). The closing reflections bring the reader to the door of the meeting place and then, consistently, stop just short of completing the entry.

The final version must complete the entry.
